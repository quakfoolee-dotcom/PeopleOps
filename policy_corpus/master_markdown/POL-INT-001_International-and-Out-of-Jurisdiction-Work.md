## INT-1. Purpose

This policy manages legal, tax, payroll, immigration, information-security, insurance, client, and operational risks when an employee proposes to work outside the province, state, or country recorded as the employee's Registered Home Office.

Approval is case-specific, revocable, and not guaranteed. Approval does not change the employee's employment location, payroll jurisdiction, compensation, benefits, or right to work unless a separate written agreement says so.

## INT-2. Scope

The policy applies whenever an employee performs any work outside the registered province or state, including email, meetings, coding, customer support, or document access during personal travel. It applies to domestic and international locations. Pure vacation with no work is not covered, but **POL-PTO-001** still applies.

## INT-3. Definitions

- **Domestic out-of-jurisdiction work:** Work in another province or state within the employee's payroll country.
- **International work:** Work in a country other than the employee's payroll country.
- **Business day worked:** Any day on which the employee performs more than incidental work, regardless of hours.
- **Standard-Risk location:** A location currently permitted for routine review under the country-risk framework.
- **Enhanced-Review location:** A location requiring additional Legal, Security, insurance, client, or export-control assessment.
- **Prohibited location:** A location from which Northstar does not permit work because required controls or legal conditions cannot be met.

## INT-4. Baseline eligibility

The employee must normally:

- be RFT or RPT and have completed 90 calendar days of service;
- be meeting performance and conduct expectations;
- have a role that can be performed from the proposed location;
- use company-managed equipment and approved connectivity;
- maintain required time-zone overlap and customer coverage;
- have no client, licensing, export-control, or data-residency restriction that prevents the arrangement; and
- demonstrate a lawful basis to be present and work in the destination.

An employee is responsible for truthful travel and authorization information. Northstar does not provide personal immigration or tax advice.

## INT-5. Duration categories, notice, and normal limits

| Category | Duration | Normal advance notice | Normal decision path |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Domestic short-term | 1-10 business days in a rolling 90 days | 10 business days | Manager + People Operations acknowledgment |
| Domestic extended | 11-30 business days in a rolling 12 months | 20 business days | Manager + director + People Operations + Payroll/Tax + Security |
| International short-term | 1-20 business days in a rolling 12 months | 20 business days | Manager + director + People Operations + Security + Finance/Tax |
| International exceptional | 21-30 business days in a rolling 12 months | 30 business days | All short-term approvals + VP sponsor + Legal review |
| Assignment or location change | More than 30 business days, recurring residence, or permanent move | At least 60 calendar days | Formal mobility, transfer, or employment-location process; not a temporary-work approval |

The normal aggregate limit is 30 domestic out-of-jurisdiction business days and 20 international business days in any rolling 12-month period. An international period of 21-30 days is exceptional and requires a documented business or compelling personal rationale. Days need not be consecutive to count.

## INT-6. Required request information

A complete request must include:

1. employee ID, role, manager, and Registered Home Office;
2. destination city, region, and country;
3. arrival and departure dates and expected business days worked;
4. reason for the request and whether the travel is personal or business;
5. work schedule and time-zone overlap;
6. systems, data classifications, customers, and regulated activities involved;
7. proposed workspace, network, equipment, and emergency contact method;
8. immigration or work-authorization basis known to the employee; and
9. any prior out-of-jurisdiction days in the rolling 12-month period.

An incomplete request may be returned for clarification rather than denied.

## INT-7. Review factors

Approvers assess, as applicable:

- immigration and permission to work;
- income-tax withholding, payroll registration, social contributions, and corporate permanent-establishment risk;
- employment standards, licensing, sanctions, export controls, and local registration;
- health, travel, workers' compensation, and insurance coverage;
- cybersecurity, data residency, government access, and device inspection risk;
- customer contracts and Restricted-data access;
- time-zone coverage, productivity, and emergency support; and
- the cumulative duration and pattern of prior approvals.

No single approver may waive another function's legal or security requirement.

## INT-8. Country-risk framework

For this synthetic policy corpus, the following examples apply. Security and Legal may change classifications at any time.

| Classification | Examples | General treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Standard-Risk | Canada, United States, European Economic Area including Germany, United Kingdom, Switzerland, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Singapore, South Korea | Eligible for normal review; approval still required |
| Enhanced Review | Any destination not listed as Standard-Risk or Prohibited | Additional Security and Legal review; controls may include restricted access or shorter duration |
| Prohibited | A destination identified by Security or Legal as subject to sanctions, active conflict, unacceptable cyber risk, or inability to support employees | Work is not approved |

A location's classification does not confirm immigration permission or tax acceptability.

## INT-9. Information-security conditions

**POL-SEC-001** applies at all times. International approvals normally require:

- company-managed device, current patches, disk encryption, MFA, and company VPN;
- no use of shared or public computers;
- no printing or local storage of Restricted information;
- private workspace and secure device storage;
- immediate reporting of device loss, border inspection, credential concerns, or suspected compromise; and
- explicit Security approval before accessing Restricted data outside the payroll country.

Security may issue a travel device, restrict applications, disable downloads, or require password changes after return.

## INT-10. Conduct, representation, and local activity

An employee must not, without specific written authority:

- sign contracts or create a local business presence;
- hire or manage local workers outside an approved structure;
- represent that Northstar has an office in the destination;
- change the payroll address or employment location based only on temporary approval;
- perform regulated work requiring a local license; or
- continue working after approval expires.

## INT-11. Costs, insurance, and emergencies

Personal-travel costs are not reimbursable. Business costs require preapproval under **POL-EXP-001**. The employee is responsible for personal travel documents and personal tax advice unless the request is a company-directed assignment.

An employee who cannot return as planned must contact the manager and People Operations before continuing work. An emergency extension is not automatic; work may be suspended until required approvals are obtained.

## INT-12. Decision outcomes

A request may be:

- **Approved** for the stated dates and conditions;
- **Approved with conditions**, such as reduced duration, limited systems, specific hours, or a travel device;
- **Returned for clarification**;
- **Escalated** for Legal, Security, tax, client, or executive review; or
- **Not approved** when risks cannot be reasonably controlled.

The decision and conditions are recorded in the HR case system. A material change in destination, dates, duties, data, or travel status requires a new review.

## INT-13. Example - six weeks in Germany

A British Columbia employee asking to work from Germany for six calendar weeks would normally propose about 30 business days. Germany is a Standard-Risk example, but the duration falls in the **International exceptional** category. The employee should submit the request at least 30 business days in advance. Approval would require the manager, director, People Operations, Security, Finance/Tax, a VP sponsor, and Legal review. The employee must also demonstrate a lawful basis to work, comply with security controls, maintain required time-zone overlap, and remain within cumulative limits. The request is not automatically approved merely because Germany is Standard-Risk.

## INT-14. Responsibilities and records

Employees submit complete, accurate requests and follow all conditions. Managers assess business feasibility and coverage. People Operations coordinates the review and retains the decision. Security, Finance/Tax, Legal, and client owners assess risks within their authority. Questions or disputed outcomes may be documented under **POL-HRC-001**, but an appeal does not authorize work while review is pending.
